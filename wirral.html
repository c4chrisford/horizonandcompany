From Thornton Hough and Heswall to West Kirby, Hoylake and the wider coastline, the Wirral offers a mix of elegant venues and open landscapes within a relatively compact area.

01 Country & Character Venues

Thornton Hough & Rural Wirral

Country venues around Thornton Hough and the wider Wirral give wedding days room to move between gardens, ceremony spaces, drinks receptions and evening celebrations.

Those changes in setting work well on film, but the venue should never overpower the people. The architecture and grounds establish where the day happened; the story comes from the interactions inside it.

Elegant surroundings without losing the atmosphere.
02 The Wirral Coast

Thurstaston, West Kirby & Hoylake

The Wirral coast has a completely different character from nearby Liverpool or inland Cheshire. Open skies, sea views and changing weather give the film a natural sense of movement.

A few carefully chosen establishing shots are usually enough to make the coastline feel part of the story without turning the wedding into an extended portrait session.

Open skies. Natural light. A strong sense of place.

Documentary Wedding Videography

The day is bigger than the two of you. That is part of what film can keep.

Some of the best moments happen while you're looking somewhere else.

A wedding film is not only the ceremony, portraits and first dance. It is the reaction during a speech, friends raising a glass, family laughing together or somebody quietly watching from the other side of the room.

I look for those moments throughout the day and build them into the finished film, so you can experience parts of your wedding you may have completely missed at the time.

The cinematic side comes from composition, movement, sound, colour and the edit itself rather than constantly directing the people in front of the camera.
